## Quarter 2 Project Reflections

**1. What is the most interesting topic covered in your domain this quarter?**

I believe that the most interesting topic covered in my domain this quarter is how mountain terrains can actually influence weather patterns. More specifically, we learned about how wind flow and extreme rainfall is affected. This is quite interesting to me because it turns out that mountains can affect where moisture can travel, as well as where precipitation occurs, which can potentially cause great differences in the rain patterns of coastal and inland regions. In understanding the relationships here, it is essential for the county of San Diego as extreme rainfall events are often contributing to flooding. On top of that, this topic is interesting in that we are able to expand our horizons/disciplines by combining meteorological datasets to understand complex weather patterns.

**2. Describe a potential investigation you would like to pursue for your Quarter 2 Project.**

A potential investigation I would like to pursue in my Quarter 2 Project is investigating whether or not particular wind directions during said extreme rainfall events are typically associated with the greater precipitation on the windward sides of mountains as opposed to leeward sides. This would be extremely interesting and useful in our project as examining patterns over multiple historical rainfall events can be beneficial, rather than focusing on only one specific event (like the January 22, 2024 storm of San Diego). In this investigation, I would also probably look into rainfall gauge observations, wind data, and information about terrain elevation to compare different weather stations in San Diego County. With this investigation, I would like to ultimately identify if those relationships are consistent to explain regional differences in extreme rainfall intensities.

**3. What is a potential change you'd make to the approach taken in your current Quarter 1 Project?**

I think one potential change I would make to the approach taken in my current Quarter 1 Project is to prioritize integrating multiple datasets together a lot earlier. Currently, I am analyzing these datasets individually. A lot of our initial work for the project really involved exploring separate sources for meteorological data in order to understand the structure and availability of the data. This is still useful in that my teammates and I were able to familiarize ourselves well with the different datasets; however, combining the information sooner could help identify relationships between variables more effectively. On top of that, it would be beneficial to determine a more systematic method of selecting "extreme rainfall events" and comparing those observations over the many different weather stations (locations).

**4. What other techniques would you be interested in using in your project?**

Other techniques that I might be interested in using in my project is applying geospatial analysis and statistical modeling in order to more easily understand the relationship between terrain/wind patterns and rainfall intensity. An example of this could be using geographic visualizations to illustrate how precipitation is different based on the elevation of a location. Another example could be wind-vector plots illustrating how airflow can change over different mountainous regions. On top of that, it could be good to apply regression models in order to examine relationships between rainfall, wind direction, wind speed, and elevation. All of these techniques can help us expand outward from simply using descriptive visualizations and quantitatively investigating all variables associated with extreme rainfall.
